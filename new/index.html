01

The programme

New capabilities change the decisions we face.

AI can make an idea practical to try before an organisation has agreed what to do with it. That creates questions about the work itself: what becomes worth attempting, what people come to expect, and who will keep it useful.

MISO gives those questions a practical setting. Work directly with AI, compare approaches with people from other fields, and use what happens to reconsider the choices in front of you.

Q1

What could we now attempt?

Q2

What would that change for people?

Q3

What would it take to keep it useful?

02

Who joins

Bring your experience. Bring an open question.

The camp brings together public leaders, policy practitioners, designers, researchers and people shaping how organisations work. Participants have different levels of AI experience and learn from seeing how others approach unfamiliar problems.

You might arrive with a service to rethink, a decision to explore or a sense that something new is possible. You do not need a finished project brief. We help you find a useful place to begin.

Everyone works directly with the tools, individually and with others.

03

How it works

Build, reflect and try again.

Two days of guided experiments, short explanations and discussion. The emphasis and preparation are shaped with each host around the people in the room.

01

Find a starting point.

Choose a question or piece of work. Identify what you want to learn and what would make a first attempt useful.

02

Make an attempt.

Work with AI to build a small prototype or explore a workflow. Notice where the result differs from what you expected.

03

Make the reasoning visible.

Describe the assumptions, inputs and judgments behind your approach. Let others question it and use what you learn in another attempt.

04

Reconsider the wider work.

Discuss what the experiment changes: who would use it, what people would expect, where human judgment matters and what ongoing work it would create.

Leave with practical experience, a record of your approach and limitations, and a clearer next decision: what to develop, what to change or where to stop.

04

The method

You cannot delegate what you cannot articulate.

Working with AI brings gaps in our instructions and assumptions into view. Making enough of the method explicit gives us a basis for checking the result and discussing it with others.

Some knowledge remains tied to experience, relationships and context. Part of the work is recognising where a person needs to stay involved, and why.

05

An example from our practice

Facilitator example · exploratory prototype

What if public information started with your circumstances?

After a conversation about a childcare rule he had missed, Igor built a prototype that organises public decisions around a resident's situation and neighbourhood.

Trying the idea made a different public-service expectation tangible: tell me what matters where I live. It also exposed the work needed to check explanations, keep information current and handle corrections.

Host a camp

Bring MISO to your institution.

We work with universities, public institutions and organisations to shape a camp around a cohort and the questions it faces. MISO can stand on its own or contribute a practical component to an executive, leadership or professional development programme.

Start with who you want to bring together and what decisions they are facing. We can then discuss the focus, preparation, tool access and support needed for that group.
