Miso Coach will help you take one piece of your work apart. Then decide what an agent takes over and where you stay in.

01

Why

You cannot delegate what you cannot articulate.

Most of how we work is tacit. We know how to open the jar, so we never write it down, and a description of our work assumes everything a grown-up would know. That is what the sandwich exercise on day one showed: written for a partner who follows the instructions exactly as written, it goes wrong at the steps nobody thought to mention.

An agent is that partner. It does not share the assumptions, and it does the wrong thing at exactly the step that was left out.

Asked for a workflow cold, people give the tidy version. The time goes into waiting, chasing, rework and exceptions, and none of that shows up because none of it feels like work. What people can describe with the gaps filled in is last Tuesday.

Miso Coach is an interview that starts from last Tuesday. It takes one thing you do regularly, finds the last time you did it, and works down from the goal to the activities, one question at a time. Then it asks, activity by activity, where you need to stay in.

02

The process

One piece of work, taken apart.

03

Three moves

The first three skills on the camp’s list, done with you.

1

See it as a workflow

What the thing is for, once it is done. Then everything that happened the last time, past tense, in any order, including the waiting and the chasing. Then the order, and the breaks between the parts. The parts are the subgoals, and under each one the activities: what you opened, wrote, sent, checked.

2

Inputs and outputs

For each activity: what you needed to start it, where that came from, what it produced, who got it. A supplier you cannot name is a missing step. An output nobody receives is a step that can go. An input that arrived late is where the time went.

3

Decide where you stay in

Each activity gets a rung on a five-step ladder, from I do it to it does, I never see it. Keeping one needs a reason, and four count: the consequence is hard to reverse; there is no ground truth, only judgment; someone needs to know a person did it; it is where your own signal comes from. Habit does not count.

04

What you leave with

A clearer goal, and the method that gets you there.

Your way of working, written down. Goal, subgoals, activities with what each needs and produces, a rung and a reason per activity, what stays with you, what gets built. In your words throughout. It is the thing you can hand to any agent afterwards, and the thing a colleague can read and disagree with.

The goal, restated. At the start you can only state the goal from inside the work as it is now. At the end, knowing which activities you hand off and which you keep, you can say what you are actually working for. What someone keeps says what they think the work is for, better than anything they said going in. If the two goals match, nothing new was found, which is a fair result. If they differ, the difference is the result.

One thing built. If an activity sits high enough on the ladder, Miso Coach builds it: it takes the activity’s inputs and outputs from the card, asks how you would know the result was good before anything is built, and builds against that. One activity per session. If nothing clears the bar, it says so, and you still leave with the method written down.

The second pass. After the built thing has run on a real instance, come back. Automating an activity does not remove it from the work; it changes the work around it, and the tree gets redrawn.

05

Where it stands

In development. Not yet run on a real participant.

Two front ends run the same interview. A Claude Code skill that writes the method to disk and builds one activity from it, and a prototype connector for Claude that fills the tree in on screen as you talk.
