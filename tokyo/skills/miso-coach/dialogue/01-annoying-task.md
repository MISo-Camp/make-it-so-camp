# 01 — The annoying task

## Purpose

Select one worthwhile work or project pain and anchor it in a real, recent occurrence. The participant walks in with a complaint, a request, or a vague frustration. This question turns it into a concrete episode with a trigger, actors, a timeline, observable consequences, and enough specificity to ground everything that follows.

## Method

### Entry

Deliver the early promise from SKILL.md — in your own words, the four ideas: work/project workflow subject, one real occurrence, no-code is possible, this produces a specification not a built product. Then move directly to the first question.

### Selecting a target

If the participant names one clear work pain, accept it without forcing candidate generation. Do not invent alternatives they did not mention.

If several pains compete, compare them conversationally:

- How often does each one happen?
- What does each one cost in time, rework, delay, or missed outcomes?
- Which one involves inspectable work states rather than just people problems?
- Which one frustrates them most?

The participant chooses. Do not rank for them.

### Anchoring in a real occurrence

Once a target is selected, anchor it. The primary prompt is:

> "Take me to the last time this happened. What kicked it off?"

After their first answer lands, ask where it became annoying.

Then follow the episode to capture the anchor evidence. Find out (one question at a time, in whatever order the conversation takes):

- **Trigger and approximate date:** what started the episode and roughly when
- **Current task or workaround:** what the participant actually did (not what they wish they'd done)
- **Who and what were involved:** people, systems, data sources, tools, approvals
- **Where the pain appeared:** the specific moment, step, wait, rework, or gap that made this annoying
- **Perceived ending and actual ending:** when the participant thought they were done versus when the work was truly finished
- **Frequency or recurrence:** how often this happens — weekly, monthly, per-project, per-client
- **Observable consequence:** what went wrong, was late, cost extra, got missed, or remained at risk

Use the probe ladder from INTERVIEW.md (open first, then referential, evidence, slot, contrast, counterfactual — only as needed). Skip any of the above that the participant has already supplied.

**Do not ask what they want to automate.** The question is about what happened, not what they want built.

### Scope check

If the described pain is not a work or project workflow — for example, it is a personal habit, life planning, motivation, or accountability — explain the mismatch using the boundary response from SKILL.md and stop `out-of-scope`.

If the pain involves a protected domain (medical, legal, financial decision-making), it may be traced only when the proposed software leaves the protected judgment with an appropriately authorized person. **Preempt the participant's anxiety** by stating this boundary early:

> "Since this involves [legal/medical/financial] work, anything we identify would leave that professional judgment entirely with you. We're only looking at the mechanical work around it."

This safety frame costs nothing and prevents the most common trust failure with protected-domain participants — the fear that you will propose automating their professional judgment.

### Insufficient case

If the participant cannot reconstruct a recent or comparable occurrence well enough to establish a trigger, actors, pain point, and ending, stop `needs-case`. Save whatever has been approved, name the missing evidence, and provide a precise resume phrase:

> "When you have a specific recent example of [the pain], come back with that and we'll pick up here."

## Exit

The macro-question is complete when:

1. One in-scope work or project pain is selected.
2. One recent or comparable occurrence is approved with at least: trigger, approximate timing, current task, who/what involved, where the pain appeared, frequency or recurrence, and observable consequence.

If met, update the case card with the approved anchor evidence and deliver the momentum checkpoint per INTERVIEW.md — one discovery, what comes next, no praise.

Then advance to `dialogue/02-job.md`.
