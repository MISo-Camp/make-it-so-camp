# 02 — The job

## Purpose

Separate the desired progress from the current task, tool, or workaround. The participant described what happened; this question asks what they were trying to make true by doing all of that. The result is a provisional job story and an observable project goal, both explicitly marked provisional until workflow evidence can test them in question 5.

## Method

### Constructing the provisional job story

From the anchor evidence approved in question 1, draft a provisional job story. **When the participant's opening was rich** — already containing clear desired progress, subgoal-like outcomes, or activity-level detail — draft the job story directly from what they already said rather than re-eliciting it through generic prompts. Acknowledge what they supplied:

> "You gave me a lot to work with. Here's a first pass at the underlying job — tell me what's off."

When the opening was sparse or solution-focused, use the standard prompts below.

Draft in this shape:

> "When [situation], I need to [make this progress], so I can [observable outcome], while preserving [important quality]."

The participant is not required to write in this format. You do the modeling; they correct your draft. Label your draft explicitly as provisional:

> "Here's a first pass at the underlying job, from what you told me about [anchor occurrence]. Tell me what's off."

Read the draft, then ask for correction. See `references/jtbd.md` for the three-object distinction (current task, job, project goal) and the quality checks.

### Useful probes

Use these when the answer still names a tool, current step, or overly abstract goal. One at a time, not as a list:

- "What were you trying to make true by doing all of that?"
- "What became possible once it was finished?"
- "If this task disappeared but you still got that result, would that satisfy you?"
- "What must a better version not damage or lose?"
- "What would be observably different if this improved?"

### WHY-up / HOW-down

When the participant's answer still names a current tool or step as the goal, use one or two WHY-up probes to lift toward the desired progress:

> "Why does that matter? What does getting it right make possible?"

When the result is too abstract to observe or test, use one HOW-down probe to ground it:

> "How would you know that had actually happened?"

Do not ladder mechanically. One or two rounds is usually enough. Stop as soon as the answer names progress that is independent of the current solution and recognizable enough to test later.

### Two objects to record

This macro-question records two distinct things:

1. **Job:** the stable desired progress in a situation, independent of the present solution. It names what the participant is trying to make true, not the steps they currently take.
2. **Project goal:** the concrete, observable workflow change this intervention should improve. This is more specific than the job — it names what would look different in the workflow if things got better.

Both are provisional at this stage. They will be tested against the full workflow and allocation decisions in question 5.

### Readback

Before exiting, read back both the provisional job story and the project goal. Label them provisional. Ask for correction:

> "Does that capture what you're actually trying to get done here? We'll revisit this after we see the full workflow."

The participant approves as "true enough to continue." This is not a permanent lock-in.

### Red flags

- Do not install a goal. If the participant's desired progress is unclear, keep it unclear and labeled as such rather than substituting your own reading.
- Do not claim to reveal a hidden "true goal." You are helping them construct and test a useful account of the progress they want.
- The job story must contain no tool, vendor, interface, or current workflow step unless it is a genuine binding constraint. If the draft includes one, ask whether the constraint is real or just the current solution.

## Exit

The macro-question is complete when:

1. An approved provisional job story exists that names a situation, desired progress, observable outcome, and (when relevant) an important quality to preserve.
2. An approved provisional project goal names the observable workflow change sought.
3. Both are explicitly marked provisional until question 5.
4. The job story passes the quality checks in `references/jtbd.md`: names a circumstance, names progress not a feature, names an observable outcome, preserves a quality when one matters, and is recognized by the participant rather than merely accepted.

Update the case card with the provisional job story, project goal, and `Goal status: provisional`. Deliver the momentum checkpoint per INTERVIEW.md.

Then advance to `dialogue/03-subgoals.md`.
