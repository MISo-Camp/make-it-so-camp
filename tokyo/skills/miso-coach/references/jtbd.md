# Jobs-to-be-Done Contract

JTBD is the participant-facing framing grammar. It is not a claim that MISO has discovered an objectively real or permanent goal.

## Three distinct objects

| Object | Example | Stability |
|---|---|---|
| Current task or solution | schedule a meeting | volatile; one present means |
| Job | get a decision from three people while the decision is still useful | comparatively stable desired progress |
| Project goal | reduce the median decision turnaround from four days to one day without bypassing required approvers | observable intervention target |

The final agent specification is governed by the job and project goal. It may implement a different solution from the current task.

- **Current task/solution** is what the participant does now. It changes whenever they change tools or workarounds. It is evidence about the job, not the job itself.
- **Job** is the stable desired progress the participant is trying to make in a recurring situation. It names no tool, vendor, or current workflow step unless that element is a binding constraint.
- **Project goal** is the observable workflow change this specific intervention should produce. It is narrower than the job and measurable enough that success or failure can be judged.

## Two-pass formulation

### Pass one (Question 2)

Create a provisional job story from the anchor occurrence:

> "When [situation], I need to [make this progress], so I can [observable outcome], while preserving [important quality]."

The participant does not write in framework syntax. MISO constructs the candidate from evidence, labels it provisional, and asks for correction.

### Pass two (Question 5)

Retest the provisional story against the full workflow, the causal bottleneck, and the human work the participant chooses to retain:

> "You came in wanting [provisional outcome]. Looking at what actually happened and what you want to keep, does that still name the progress you want?"

The second pass may:

- **Confirm** the provisional story unchanged.
- **Narrow** it to a more specific circumstance or outcome.
- **Broaden** it when the workflow revealed a larger pattern.
- **Replace** it when the evidence contradicts the original framing.

An unchanged story is a valid result. When the second pass changes what the workflow must accomplish, MISO reopens Question 3 or 4 as required and re-approves traceability before allocation or candidate selection continues.

## Job-story quality checks

The approved story must pass all six:

1. **Names a circumstance or trigger** — not a permanent state or vague desire.
2. **Names desired progress rather than a feature** — describes what becomes true, not what software does.
3. **Names an observable or recognizable outcome** — the participant or another person can tell when it has happened.
4. **Preserves a critical quality when one matters** — names what a better version must not damage or lose.
5. **Contains no tool, vendor, interface, or current workflow step** — unless that element is a binding constraint the participant confirms.
6. **Is recognized by the participant** — not merely accepted as a tidy paraphrase. The participant should say "yes, that's what I mean," not "sure, that sounds fine."

## Evidence boundary

The supplied literature treats JTBD mainly as practitioner material rather than a validated elicitation intervention. MISO therefore uses JTBD for comprehensible phrasing while grounding its elicitation mechanics in:

- concrete-case reconstruction (replay a real occurrence);
- structured interviewing (systematic probes, one question per turn);
- clean questions (minimize interviewer framing);
- guided discovery (participant-owned reframing); and
- WHY-up/HOW-down goal refinement (ladder only when the answer still names a tool or is too abstract to observe).

JTBD is framing syntax. It gives the participant a recognizable sentence they can correct. The analytical work underneath is concrete-case evidence and structured decomposition.
